To European and international film industry organizations:

On Tuesday, March 18, 2026, Gender Watchdog sent a formal briefing to the EU Delegation to the Republic of Korea, documenting systemic Gender Equality Plan compliance failures among Korean institutions currently in the Horizon Europe research pipeline.[^1]

On Thursday, March 19, 2026 — within one business day — Rainer Wessely, Counsellor for Digital and Research at the EU Delegation in Seoul, confirmed receipt and informed Gender Watchdog that the briefing had been forwarded to EC DG Research & Innovation — specifically the units dealing with international partnerships and gender equality — with a formal commitment to verify whether active Horizon Europe proposals are in conformity with GEP requirements.[^2]

On February 18, 2026 — thirty-six days before today — Gender Watchdog sent an equivalent formal notice to the Canadian Media Producers Association (CMPA), documenting institutional risk in its December 17, 2025 MOU with the Producers Guild of Korea (PGK). That notice gave CMPA seven days to respond.[^3]

CMPA has not responded. Not by the February 25 deadline. Not in the three weeks following our March 11 public escalation to press, Indigenous screen organizations, government, and Cannes.[^4] Not today.

The EU is verifying. CMPA is silent.What the EU Has Been Asked to Verify

The briefing forwarded by Mr. Wessely to EC DG Research & Innovation concerns documented Gender Equality Plan non-compliance among Korean institutions in the Horizon Europe pipeline — the same institutional ecosystem that produces and distributes the Korean film and drama content now reaching European audiences through platforms including Kyool.[^5]

Chung-Ang University (CAU): Korea's oldest theatre and film program (est. 1959), the country's only graduate film school running all four phases of the government's BK21 elite research program, and a direct institutional pipeline into CJ ENM and Netflix content production. CAU's professor Kisung Ko holds the formally government-appointed role of Korea's National Contact Point (NCP) for Horizon Europe Clusters 1 and 6 — Health and Bioeconomy, the two largest spending clusters in Pillar II.[^6] CAU has no published Gender Equality Plan. An independent audit of its claimed international partnerships returned a 27% reciprocity rate: only 13 of 49 checkable claimed partner universities were confirmed by the partners' own records. Twenty-one years of documented sexual violence cover-ups across academic departments, spanning 1998 to 2019. Zero permanent sanctions.[^7]

Dongguk University: The only institution among the twelve in the K-ERC June 2025 Horizon Europe forum to file a GEP — and it was filed in December 2025, the same month Gender Watchdog's multi-language documentation of 34 falsified international partnerships reached universities and media globally.[^8] The document fails four substantive compliance standards set by the European Institute for Gender Equality: no survivor reporting mechanism, no independent oversight, no student participation in formation, and no sex-disaggregated data for arts and culture students — precisely the population the Korean Women's Development Institute's government study (2020) found carries the highest documented sexual violence risk in Korean higher education, with 61.5% of female arts and culture students reporting sexual violence and film and media departments scoring 81/100 on structural risk indicators.[^1]

Dongguk's campus shares a building with Sidus FNH, the entertainment production company whose founder Tcha Seung-jai — convicted of bid-rigging while a professor at Dongguk — was subsequently promoted to Dean of the Film & Video Production Department in 2023.[^9] This is the institutional pipeline the CMPA-PGK MOU formalizes engagement with.

The Korean government's own research quantifies the structural risk: the Korean Women's Development Institute's 2020 study found 61.5% of female arts and culture students and 17.2% of male students experience sexual violence in Korean university arts programs, with 66% of all cases across disciplines perpetrated by faculty. Film and media departments carried the highest documented risk in the study — 81/100 on structural risk indicators in Gender Watchdog's analysis of the KWDI data.[^11]The Pattern Extends Across Ranking and Oversight Bodies

On March 24, 2026 — in the same 24-hour window that Pusan National University deleted its entire international partner page from the internet — Times Higher Education responded to nine documented cases of Korean university partnership fraud with three sentences, directing Gender Watchdog to the Korean Ministry of Education.[^10]

The Ministry of Education was formally notified by Gender Watchdog on April 10, 2025. As of today — eleven months and fifteen days later — no public corrective action has been taken.

THE's verbatim response confirmed in writing that its due-diligence framework for Korean universities is a pass-through to MOE accreditation — and that MOE accreditation does not require reciprocal verification of the partnership claims used to calculate international standing scores. This is now a documented institutional admission, published in full.

The institutional deference loop is confirmed across three separate bodies: the world's leading rankings organization defers to the accreditation regulator, the accreditation regulator has not acted on eleven months of formal notification, and the research funding framework is now in active verification of the same underlying failures.Why This Matters to European Film Industry Organizations

The Korean film and drama industry's international prestige — the prestige that makes co-production agreements commercially attractive to CMPA, that makes Korean content viable for European distribution platforms like Kyool, and that drives the partnership frameworks European film promotion bodies increasingly engage with at Busan, Cannes, and Berlin — rests on institutional credentials that are now under documented challenge from three directions simultaneously.

Specifically:The academic institutions that train Korea's film and drama talent do not meet the baseline gender equality standards required for European research funding partnerships.[^6]The industry figures listed as senior faculty at those institutions include a convicted criminal promoted to Dean.[^9]The ranking bodies that certify those institutions' global standing have confirmed in writing that they do not independently verify the international partnership claims driving those scores.[^10]The regulatory body responsible for all of this — Korea's Ministry of Education — has not acted in eleven months and fifteen days despite formal notification.[^3]

France's 327-0 consent law, Sweden's 50/50 gender parity legacy in film, the BFI's co-production equity standards, the EU's own Horizon Europe Gender Equality Plan mandate: each of these frameworks now sits in direct structural tension with business-as-usual engagement with the Korean film and drama ecosystem as currently constituted. The Korean government's own data is the baseline: 61.5% of female arts and culture students experience sexual violence in Korean universities. 66% of all cases across disciplines are perpetrated by faculty. Film and media departments — the institutions training the talent entering European co-production pipelines — carry the highest documented risk score in the government study.[^11]

Four months before Park Chan-wook's Cannes appointment was announced, France passed its 327-0 consent law redefining rape as any sexual act without freely given consent. The jury presiding over Cannes 2026 represents an industry where equivalent consent reform has been proposed multiple times and has not passed. The MOU formalizing Canadian producer engagement with the pipeline behind that content was signed December 17, 2025 — and the Canadian industry representative with the formal obligation to address the documented risks has not responded.A Note for Canadian Recipients
