To members of the film press, Indigenous screen sovereignty organizations, government oversight bodies, and regional film industry associations:

Three weeks ago, on February 18, 2026, Gender Watchdog — supported by End Rape On Campus (EROC) — sent a formal notice to the Canadian Media Producers Association (CMPA) regarding documented human rights risks embedded in its December 17, 2025 MOU with the Producers Guild of Korea (PGK).

That notice gave CMPA seven days to respond before we released our findings publicly. The deadline was February 25, 2026.

CMPA did not respond. Not by the deadline, and not in the three weeks since.

One day after that deadline, on February 26, 2026, the Cannes Film Festival announced that South Korean director Park Chan-wook would preside over the jury of its 79th edition — the first Korean director in Cannes history to hold this role.

We have now published our full findings. We are writing to ensure you have access to the documentation.

5. Cannes Is a French Institution. France Just Voted 327-0.

On October 29, 2025, the French Senate voted 327-0 for a consent-based rape law redefining rape as any sexual act committed without consent that is "freely given, informed, specific, prior and revocable."

France's own film industry drove part of this change. Judith Godrèche stood at the 49th César Awards in February 2024 and told a room of industry colleagues — and millions of viewers: "Why allow this art that we love so much... to be used as a cover for the illicit trafficking of young girls? We can decide that men accused of rape should not be allowed to call the shots in cinema." France's legislature responded.

Cannes itself barred an accused perpetrator from the red carpet in 2025 while sexual assault allegations remained under appeal — the first such exclusion in its 78-year history, following sustained pressure and Gérard Depardieu's conviction.

South Korea's equivalent consent reform was proposed by its Ministry of Gender in January 2023 and cancelled by the Ministry of Justice within hours. Multiple bills have since been introduced in the National Assembly. None has passed.

Park Chan-wook's Cannes appointment was announced three months after France's 327-0 vote. His sole public statement on the Korean film industry's sexual violence crisis — made at a Cine21 forum in 2016 — was that he "was under the impression that the Korean film industry had cleaned up well compared to before." That statement predates the KWDI's 61.5% finding, three deaths in two years, the Burning Sun exposure, Japan's unanimous consent reform, France's 327-0 vote, and Dongguk's promotion of a convicted professor to Dean.

As a French institution whose home country just redefined accountability, Cannes has considerably less institutional cover for this appointment than it did before.

6. The Statistical Baseline: 61.5%

The Korean Women's Development Institute (KWDI) 2020 report — a Korean government-linked research body — documented a 61.5% sexual violence rate among female students in Korean university arts and culture programs. Film departments scored the highest risk rating (81/100) of all academic disciplines surveyed. 66% of all campus sexual violence is perpetrated by faculty.
