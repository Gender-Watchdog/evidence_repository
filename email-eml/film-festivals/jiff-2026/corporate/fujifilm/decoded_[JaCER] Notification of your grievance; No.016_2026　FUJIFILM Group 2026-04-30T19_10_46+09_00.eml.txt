【Relationship with Our Members】

Fujifilm Korea (후지필름코리아) is a Korean subsidiary of Fujifilm Holdings
 Corporation. Fujifilm Holdings joined JaCER in June 2024, publicly committing to the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights framework and to accessible grievance and remedy mechanisms across its group companies and value chain.

Fujifilm Korea is currently listed as an active corporate sponsor of the 27th Jeonju International Film Festival (JIFF) (April 29 – May 8, 2026, Jeonju, Republic of Korea). JIFF is a major Korean
 government-funded international film festival that screens Korean independent cinema produced through Korea&#039;s film education pipeline — programs at Dongguk University, Chung-Ang University (CAU), Korea National University of Arts (K-Arts), Seoul Institute
 of Arts, and others operating inside the same high-risk academic environment documented by the KWDI 2020 study.

The human rights concern is: Fujifilm Korea&#039;s active financial sponsorship of JIFF 2026 lends corporate legitimacy and resources to a festival that (a) has zero public sexual violence prevention
 policy, (b) ignored three documented formal requests for safety reform in 2025, and (c) exhibits the accumulated risk profile documented below — without any indication that Fujifilm Korea conducted human rights due diligence on JIFF&#039;s institutional framework
 prior to extending sponsorship. This constitutes a failure of supply chain human rights due diligence under Fujifilm Holdings&#039; own stated commitments.

【2-7.Case Details】

WHO: Fujifilm Korea (후지필름코리아), Korean subsidiary of Fujifilm Holdings
 Corporation (a JaCER member since June 2024), acting as a named corporate sponsor of the 27th Jeonju International Film Festival (JIFF), which opens April 29, 2026 in Jeonju, Republic of Korea.

WHAT: Fujifilm Korea is financially sponsoring a major publicly funded Korean film festival that has demonstrably zero sexual violence prevention infrastructure, has ignored documented formal
 safety reform requests, and serves as the primary exhibition venue for films produced through the highest-risk sexual violence environment in Korean higher education — without conducting or publishing any human rights due diligence assessment of the festival&#039;s
 institutional framework.

TO WHOM: Female and male students enrolled in Korean university film and arts programs who are the pipeline that produces JIFF&#039;s content; international students recruited on the basis of
 partnerships that Korean institutions have documented as fabricated; and the broader public exposed to Fujifilm Korea&#039;s brand association with this institutional environment.

WHEN AND IN WHAT WAY:

The statistical foundation — KWDI 2020:

The Korean Women&#039;s Development Institute (KWDI), a Korean government research body, found in its 2020 study that 61.5% of female students in Korean university arts and culture programs experience
 sexual violence. Male students experience a rate of 17.2%. Gender Watchdog&#039;s independent analysis of KWDI data identifies film departments as the highest-risk academic environment of any discipline examined. Source: https://eng.kwdi.re.kr/inc/download.do?ut=A&amp;upIdx=102748&amp;no=1

JIFF&#039;s zero policy — confirmed April 22, 2026:

JIFF&#039;s 2026 website returns zero results for &quot;sexual,&quot; zero for &quot;harassment,&quot; zero for
성폭력 (sexual violence), and zero for
성희롱 (sexual harassment) in either language. There is no visible sexual violence prevention policy, no anonymous reporting mechanism,
 and no code of conduct on the festival&#039;s public-facing website as of April 22, 2026.

Three ignored formal requests — April–May 2025:

Between April 16 and May 4, 2025, Gender Watchdog sent three formal emails to JIFF&#039;s organizing committee across nine staff addresses. The requests asked for: an ethical review of faculty
 participation given documented criminal convictions, a public gender equity statement, and an anonymous reporting channel for students and artists. JIFF did not respond to any email — no acknowledgment, no refusal. One year later, the policy gap is unchanged.
 Email documentation: https://github.com/genderwatchdog1/timeline-website/blob/master/imgs/email-screenshots/04162025-jiff-email.png

The Dongguk University pipeline — criminal faculty still listed:

JIFF&#039;s Korean competition content runs primarily through Dongguk University&#039;s Graduate School of Digital Image &amp; Contents. On January 28, 2026, a Dongguk University professor (age
 40) was arrested by Okayama Prefectural Police in Japan for non-consensual indecent assault against a Korean woman. As of March 26, 2026 — seven weeks after his arrest by Japanese law enforcement — he was still listed as active faculty on the university&#039;s
 official website.

The Graduate School&#039;s founding academic partner, Tcha Seung-jai (차승재)
 — co-CEO of Sidus FNH, one of Korea&#039;s top five film production companies physically located in the building where Dongguk&#039;s film department teaches — was convicted of bid-rigging by a Korean court in 2017, then promoted twice: to Research Institute
 Director in March 2020, and to 11th Dean of the Graduate School in March 2023 (confirmed by Yonhap News Agency, Korea&#039;s official state wire service). Gender Watchdog identifies this as a quadruple dependency relationship maximizing student vulnerability:
 corporate control of the production company sharing the building, academic authority over grades and progression, industry gatekeeping over employment, and industry association leadership (President, Korea Film Producers Association). Students understood that
 academic failure and industry-wide exclusion were simultaneously at risk. Source: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/from-indictment-to-deans-office-how-dongguk-university-rewarded-a-criminal-conviction-with-promotions/

Reactive cover rather than reform — GEP filing and faculty purge:

In March 2026, Dongguk University filed a Gender Equality Plan under EU Horizon Europe eligibility requirements. The task force was formed in December 2025 — the same month Gender Watchdog published
 documentation of 34 falsified international partnerships. Gender Watchdog infers the task force was formed in direct response to public exposure, not as a proactive institutional commitment. Eight days after the EU-Korea Research and Innovation Day in Seoul
 (March 24, 2026), Dongguk&#039;s Graduate School faculty roster was simultaneously altered: two female research-credentialed professors were removed entirely; the sole government-research-linked female faculty position was eliminated as a functioning unit;
 a senior male professor was stripped of all institutional contact information. The tenured and tenure-track faculty tier remains 100% male across all recorded snapshots. Source: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/dongguk-faculty-purge-paper-faculty-eu-cleanup-april-2026/

Five governmental cultural entities withdrew from JIFF 2026:

Five Western governmental cultural entities present at JIFF 2025 are absent in 2026 — confirmed from JIFF&#039;s own partners page HTML (Wayback Machine captures): Canada&#039;s federal wordmark,
 the Québec Government Office Seoul, Japan Foundation Seoul, the Australian Embassy Seoul, and the French Embassy culture section. The four nations those entities represent — Canada, Japan, France, Australia — are the exact four nations Gender Watchdog formally
 contacted about the documented sexual violence crisis in Korea&#039;s film education ecosystem. Nations GW did not contact at equivalent depth — Austria, Brazil, Spain, Peru — all remained as JIFF partners in 2026. Fujifilm Korea remained as a sponsor. Archive
 comparison: https://web.archive.org/web/20251228231945/https://eng.jeonjufest.kr/about_jiff/partner.asp (2025) vs https://web.archive.org/web/20260421232240/https://eng.jeonjufest.kr/about_jiff/partner.asp (2026).

The structural outcome — statistical erasure of foreign nationals:

Across Korean film and television — directors, screenwriters, producers, executives — there are effectively zero foreign women and zero foreign men in creative leadership positions. Not underrepresentation.
 Statistical erasure of all foreign nationals, regardless of gender, in an industry that recruits thousands of international students annually — many on the basis of international partnerships documented as fabricated (34 falsified partnerships documented,
 including UBC, which Dongguk silently deleted from its Global Partners page in January 2026 after Gender Watchdog published the discrepancy). Sources: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/the-statistical-impossibility-why-there-are-zero-foreign-women-in-korean-entertainment-leadership/
 and https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/foreign-men-in-korean-entertainment-leadership-systematic-exclusion-to-prevent-whistleblowing/

Full published documentation: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/jiff-2026-nations-that-left/

【2-9.Remedies you expect】

Gender Watchdog is not requesting that Fujifilm Korea withdraw from JIFF 2026 on 24-hour notice. We are placing this documentation in Fujifilm Holdings&#039; human rights due diligence record
 to request the following specific remedies:

1. Sponsorship review: Fujifilm Korea&#039;s JIFF participation should be assessed against Fujifilm Holdings&#039; Human Rights Policy and the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights
 before any 2027 sponsorship renewal decision is made. Future JIFF sponsorship decisions should include verification that the festival has a public sexual violence prevention policy, an anonymous reporting mechanism, and a code of conduct — as a standard, non-negotiable
 human rights due diligence criterion.

2. Supply chain human rights risk mapping: The Korean film industry&#039;s documented structural suppression mechanisms — including the academic pipeline&#039;s 61.5%/17.2% sexual violence rates,
 the quadruple dependency model, and the statistical exclusion of all foreign nationals from creative leadership — should be incorporated into Fujifilm&#039;s human rights risk mapping for the Korea market.

3. JaCER record: This filing should be processed as a formal grievance under JaCER&#039;s public mechanism and listed on JaCER&#039;s public Grievance List, consistent with Fujifilm Holdings&#039;
 own stated commitment to accessible and transparent remedy. Gender Watchdog requests acknowledgment of receipt and notice of whether Fujifilm Korea is asked to respond.

Other grievance mechanisms consulted: We are simultaneously filing with Fujifilm Holdings&#039; &quot;[FUJIFILM Holdings] Contact regarding Sustainability&quot; form (https://holdings.fujifilm.com/en/form/FH-CSR-En).
