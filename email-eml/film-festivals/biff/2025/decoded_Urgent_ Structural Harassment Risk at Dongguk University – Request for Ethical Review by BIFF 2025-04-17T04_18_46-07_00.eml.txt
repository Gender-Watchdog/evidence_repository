Dear BIFF Programming
Team,

I am writing on behalf of an anonymous civic
initiative, **Gender Watchdog**, which is coordinating a multi-region
advocacy campaign to address **systemic gender safety risks** at
**Dongguk University** (Seoul), specifically its **graduate film
program**.

Though Dongguk maintains strong ties with the
Korean and international film industries, its graduate film program
currently exhibits several unresolved structural red flags:

-
An **all-male faculty**,  

- No active **student-led gender
advocacy body** (former Women’s Council was disbanded in 2018),  

-
No **clear or transparent sexual harassment reporting system**,  

-
**Proximity to Sidus FNH**, a major Korean film production company
located in the same building, creating unsupervised access to
students.

These conditions have already triggered global
concern:

- A leading **university ranking body** is reviewing
Dongguk’s governance,

- Multiple **North American
universities** are reassessing their partnerships over **Title IX
exposure**.

🎬 We respectfully urge BIFF to:

-
**Reassess any current or future involvement** of Dongguk faculty,
alumni, or institutional affiliates in your programs,  

-
**Issue a public clarification** on your gender equity standards and
safeguarding policies,  

- Consider establishing an **anonymous
reporting mechanism** for students, staff, and artists engaging with
the festival.

In light of BIFF’s past leadership
challenges and the global spotlight on ethics in the Korean film
industry, we believe your festival has a unique opportunity to
demonstrate renewed commitment to transparency, gender equity, and
accountability in cultural programming. Proactive engagement on this
issue could set a vital precedent for the region.

We are
available to provide additional documentation or respond to media
inquiries.
