This communication is addressed to Babcock International's compliance and ESG teams as a formal notification under applicable supply chain human rights due diligence frameworks. This is Gender Watchdog's first correspondence with Babcock International.

Gender Watchdog is an independent advocacy organization documenting racialized sexual violence in Korean universities and the Korean film industry, supported by EROC (End Rape On Campus).[1] Our documentation has been acknowledged by a Canadian diplomat as a "sensitive matter" in written correspondence and overlaps with human rights concerns documented in Human Rights Watch Japan's June 2025 report on South Korea.[2][3] Our reciprocity audit across nine Korean universities has documented systematic partnership falsification β€” including 34 confirmed falsified listings at Dongguk University alone, with two Canadian universities confirming no active partnership with Dongguk β€” the University of British Columbia through a FOI records search that returned zero documents mentioning Dongguk, obtained only after a formal complaint to the BC Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner was required to compel a response, and a second Canadian institution explicitly in writing[4]; not one of the nine audited institutions has issued a rebuttal or counter-audit.[5] Gender Watchdog has seven federal Access to Information requests on file, six of which remain active across Global Affairs Canada, National Defence, and Public Services and Procurement Canada. A formal complaint has been filed with the Office of the Information Commissioner of Canada. In March 2026, EU Delegation Counsellor Rainer Wessely confirmed that our briefing on Korean university Gender Equality Plan failures had been forwarded to EC DG Research & Innovation for active verification, with EC HQ Brussels confirmed aware as of April 6.

Babcock International is an established participant in Canadian defence infrastructure with active relationships with Canada's Department of National Defence β€” which places Babcock in a position of direct visibility into the Canadian Patrol Submarine Project evaluation environment in which Hanwha Ocean has submitted an unsolicited bid.

Despite Hanwha Ocean's marketing of the KSS-III Batch II as having over 80% domestically sourced components, the critical 20% includes at least six European-manufactured systems essential to the vessel's operational capability: propulsion (3 Γ— MTU 12V 4000 U83 diesel engines β€” Rolls-Royce Power Systems AG, Germany[6]), optronic mast suite (Safran Series 30 β€” Safran Electronics & Defense, France[7]), electronic warfare support (Pegaso RESM system β€” Indra Sistemas S.A., Spain[8]), weapon handling and launch system (WHLS β€” Babcock International, UK[9]), steering consoles (ECA Group, France), and mine-avoidance sonar (Thales, France).[10]

Hanwha Ocean has submitted an unsolicited bid for Canada's Canadian Patrol Submarine Project (CPSP), a procurement estimated between $20 billion and $24 billion Canadian with a 35-year sustainment commitment, competing alongside TKMS (Germany) among others. CPSP evaluation is ongoing through summer 2026. A contract award would make European component makers long-term participants in a major NATO-aligned procurement with human rights liabilities that have now entered the formal federal oversight record in Canada.[11]

We are writing to ensure that Babcock International's ESG and compliance teams have this documentation before any further supply or partnership commitments are made in connection with Hanwha Ocean's CPSP bid.

61.5% of female students in Korean university arts and culture programs experience sexual violence β€” a direct finding of the Korean Women's Development Institute (KWDI), a South Korean government research body, in its 2020 study.[12] Among male students, the rate is 17.2%.[12:1] Gender Watchdog's independent analysis of KWDI data identifies film departments as the highest-risk academic environment of any discipline examined β€” a structural risk score of 81/100 derived from GW's own methodology applied to the KWDI data, a figure that does not appear in the KWDI report itself.[13] Korea Times separately reports that 66% of all sexual violence at Korean universities across all disciplines is perpetrated by male-dominated faculty.

The institutional architecture producing this outcome is documented at Dongguk University's Graduate School of Digital Image & Contents. Tcha Seung-jai β€” founding co-CEO of Sidus FNH, one of Korea's top five film production companies, whose offices are physically located in the same building as Dongguk's film department teaching spaces β€” was convicted of bid-rigging by a Korean court in 2017, then promoted twice: to Research Institute Director in March 2020, and to Dean of the Graduate School in March 2023.[14] Gender Watchdog's analysis identifies this as a quadruple dependency relationship maximizing student vulnerability across four simultaneous power structures: corporate control (co-CEO of the production company sharing the department's building), academic authority (faculty member controlling grades and academic progression), industry gatekeeping (controlling access to internships, employment, and the Korean film industry), and association leadership (as President of the Korea Film Producers Association, controlling industry-wide standards and professional networking).[15] Students understood that displeasing their instructor risked not only their academic career but effective industry-wide exclusion β€” a structural silence mechanism built into the architecture of the program itself.[15:1] In May 2025, Sidus's law firm issued a formal legal demand for retraction of this documentation β€” citing a claimed 15-year absence from Dongguk's campus β€” while Dongguk's own archived institutional website, as of April 2025, still explicitly states that Sidus FNH is based in the same building as the film department. Gender Watchdog published the full legal threat and filed professional misconduct complaints with bar associations across Asia, Europe, and North America, including the IBA, ABA, Bar Council UK, and Singapore Law Society.[16]

Dongguk also listed the University of British Columbia as a global partner; after the BC Office of Information and Privacy Commissioner intervened in a Gender Watchdog FOI request, Dongguk silently deleted UBC without correction or explanation.[17] In the same update, Dongguk replaced "Toronto Metropolitan University" β€” the correct legal name since April 2022 β€” with "Ryerson University," the name that institution retired four years earlier. An institution maintaining an active exchange relationship with TMU would be corresponding with @torontomu.ca addresses. The dead-name reversion establishes that Dongguk was copying from records so outdated they predate April 2022. Administrative errors are corrected with announcements. Fraud is corrected with silence and deletion.[17:1]

The architecture of falsification continued evolving under monitoring. In May 2026, monitoring software detected all six continental partner page tabs simultaneously stripped of navigation and replaced with a new CMS template β€” a technique Gender Watchdog terms "CMS orphaning": content preserved at original URLs, navigation removed, replacement published at a fresh address. The new partner hub, /page/1543, presents an interactive world map of 359 institutional partners. Three Canadian spot-checks against partner institutions' own live databases: the University of Ottawa appears as a new Canadian partner β€” not present in Dongguk's previous partner architecture β€” yet U Ottawa's own student exchange database returns zero results for Dongguk across 87 listed destinations; the University of Manitoba no longer appears in Dongguk's partner hub at all, having confirmed in writing to Gender Watchdog in spring 2025 that no exchange agreement with Dongguk exists, with its own database now reflecting that reality; Toronto Metropolitan University is now correctly named on /page/1543 β€” Dongguk had deleted TMU in January 2026, reverted to the dead name "Ryerson" under that scrub, then restored the correct name under subsequent pressure, completing a double inversion β€” but the URL that TMU's own database publicly lists for the partnership returns Dongguk's own 404 error page.[18] The University of Ottawa's addition warrants specific note: Ottawa is Canada's capital and the seat of the Canadian Patrol Submarine Project procurement decision for which Hanwha Ocean has submitted a bid. Whether the addition is coincidental or deliberate, it did not survive a ten-minute reciprocity check.

Dongguk is not an isolated case. Gender Watchdog has deployed automated change-detection software (Visualping) and web archive monitoring (Wayback Machine) across a sample of Korean university international partnership pages. Between January and March 2026, five institutions took their partnership databases offline or blank in rapid succession: Seoul National University, Chung-Ang University, Sogang University, Hongik University, and Ajou University. Each database was later restored, or in Sogang's case has remained dark. Not one institution issued a rebuttal, correction, or counter-audit in response to our methodology.[19]

Across nine institutions, Gender Watchdog's reciprocity audit β€” verifying each claimed partnership against the named institution's own public database β€” has documented claimed partners that those institutions do not confirm: Fudan University, Beijing Foreign Studies University, Sun Yat-sen University, and East China Normal University at Keimyung; Universitas Indonesia at both Chungbuk National University and Catholic University of Korea; and Oslo at Dongguk, Ewha, and SNU β€” with Oslo classifying all three as faculty-level Humanities MOUs, not institution-wide student exchange agreements.[19:1] Beijing Institute of Technology has additionally confirmed in writing to Gender Watchdog that its listed relationship with a Korean partner institution is MOU-only, not a student exchange agreement. Since the publication of the nine-university audit in March 2026, Gender Watchdog's reciprocity outreach has expanded to the global top 600+ universities; three US institutions have responded to date confirming they are reviewing the agreements currently in place with Dongguk University.[20] Final results of the expanded audit remain pending a concluding manual verification pass by a human reviewer β€” findings to date indicate the scope of partnership falsification across the Korean university sector is likely broader than the published nine-institution sample. QS assigns "International Outlook" scores to these institutions based in part on this partnership data without independently verifying claimed partnerships against named partner databases. Professor John Ioannidis of Stanford University confirmed to Gender Watchdog that some institutions are "addicted to gaming" rankings.[21]

The human cost is documented in one case with a name. Tu Anh β€” a 25-year-old Keimyung University graduate in International Commerce β€” fell to her death on October 28, 2025, fleeing an immigration raid at a car parts factory in Daegu.[22][23] She was working in a factory because her "global degree" had failed to secure her a professional visa. Only 10% of foreign bachelor's degree holders in Korea obtain an E-7 professional visa; the other 90% are trapped in precarious visa status that can collapse into deportation exposure at any moment.[22:1] The elite Chinese partnerships Keimyung used to market its International Commerce degree β€” Fudan, BFSU, Sun Yat-sen, ECNU β€” were absent from every one of those institutions' own partner databases when Gender Watchdog conducted its reciprocity audit. Keimyung's mid-tier Chinese partnerships are confirmed. The elite network used to recruit students like Tu Anh was not.[19:2]

On April 1, 2026 β€” eight days after the EU-Korea Research and Innovation Day at the Four Seasons Hotel Seoul β€” automated monitoring flagged simultaneous changes to Dongguk University's Graduate School of Digital Image & Contents faculty roster.[24] Two female research visiting professors were removed entirely; the only faculty position in that tier that carried a BK21 government research funding credential was eliminated, leaving the research visiting professor tier with no government-research-linked credential of any kind. A senior male film directing professor was simultaneously transitioned to emeritus status with all four institutional contact fields stripped β€” both phone numbers, the research office designation, and the physical office room β€” leaving only a personal Naver email address.[24:1]

The tenured and tenure-track faculty tier was 100% male in the July 2025 snapshot. It remained 100% male in the April 2026 snapshot. The research visiting professor tier was the only tier where women held research-credentialed positions. As of April 1, 2026, it no longer contains a government-research-linked credential of any kind. The purge occurred eight days after the institution published a Gender Equality Plan to satisfy Horizon Europe eligibility requirements β€” the GEP filed reactively in the same month GW published 34 falsified international partnerships.[24:2]

Dongguk University published a Gender Equality Plan on March 18, 2026 β€” six days before the EU-Korea Research and Innovation Day at the Four Seasons Hotel Seoul β€” making it the only institution among all twelve Korean universities represented at that forum with a publicly accessible GEP.[28] Dongguk's GEP Task Force was formed in December 2025 β€” the same month Gender Watchdog published multi-language documentation of 34 falsified international partnerships at that institution.
