Dear President Armstrong, Ms. White, Ms. Loomis, and the Team at Ontario Shipyards,

We are copying this letter to Secretary of State Fuhr, the Department of National Defence, the Office of the Prime Minister, opposition defence critics James Bezan and Lindsay Mathyssen, and the Standing Committee on National Defence, as the concerns raised go directly to Canada's $60 billion submarine procurement evaluation.

CC note to Dr. Andrew Latham: Your August 2025 analysis in the National Security Journal is directly cited in the advocacy documentation linked below. We are grateful for your independent technical contribution to this debate.

We are writing on behalf of Gender Watchdog, an advocacy organization focused on institutional integrity and racialized sexual violence in higher education, supported by EROC (End Rape On Campus). We document governance failures and procurement risks where institutional partnerships cross into active harm — to students, to workers, and to the public interest.

This letter concerns Hanwha Ocean's Letters of Intent, signed in February 2026, with Mohawk College and Ontario Shipyards, in the context of Canada's $60 billion submarine procurement.

We are asking both institutions to commission urgent internal due diligence before proceeding.Part I — Mohawk College: A Reconciliation Obligation

Mohawk College's Strategic Plan under President Armstrong makes a "strong commitment to Truth and Reconciliation." The College is named for the Mohawk Nation, one of the founding members of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy. It operates active educational partnerships with Six Nations Polytechnic and has student programs specifically serving Indigenous learners.

We are writing to raise a structural incompatibility between those commitments and a formal partnership with Hanwha Ocean.The Cultural Record

South Korean films and stage productions — including the recently distributed "No Other Choice" — have repeatedly used First Nations and Native American war bonnets, headdresses, and ceremonial regalia as entertainment costume: worn as spectacle, deployed as visual shorthand for "the exotic other."

Gender Watchdog documented this pattern on X (Twitter thread, November 2025):

X.com Thread — Appropriation of First Nations Regalia in Korean Film: https://twitter.com/Gender_Watchdog/status/1986465927228260633

In Canada, war bonnets are sacred items in Plains First Nations traditions — earned through acts of valor and community recognition. Their appropriation in commercial entertainment is a specific violation of the spirit of TRC Call to Action 57 (education and awareness about Indigenous peoples) and the broader commitment to cultural safety in reconciliation frameworks.

This is not incidental. It is visible in the cultural exports of the company now proposing to enter a training partnership with an institution named for the Mohawk Nation.Why Self-Correction Is Structurally Foreclosed

One might ask: has Korea's cultural industry been called to account for this? Is change underway?

In 2025, South Korea's National Human Rights Commission — the government body responsible for human rights accountability — submitted a report to the United Nations Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination stating that racism does not exist in Korea.

Korean civic groups condemned this as "a serious betrayal of Korea's human rights obligations" and submitted a 20-page counter-report to the UN. Human rights lawyer Park Ji-eun responded publicly: "The claim that there is no racism in Korea is absurd. The government must face the reality that racism is both structural and normalized."

Herald Insight — "The Racism No One Wants to Talk About" (Jeongmin Kim, student journalist, 2025): https://heraldinsight.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=5516

Korea ratified the UN's ICERD treaty in 1978 — over 45 years ago — and has still not passed a national anti-discrimination law. 7 out of 10 foreign residents in Korea report experiencing discrimination based on skin color. The government denies this is happening.

When an institution committed to Truth and Reconciliation enters a training partnership with a defense contractor whose home country officially denies that racial discrimination exists — and whose entertainment ecosystem treats Indigenous ceremonial regalia as movie costume — Mohawk's own reconciliation framework is implicated.

The question for Mohawk's Board of Governors is not whether Hanwha intends any offense. The question is whether a formal institutional partnership with this company is compatible with the TRC Calls to Action Mohawk has publicly committed to uphold.

We are directing this specific section to Amanda White, Director of the Centre for Indigenous Relations, Knowledge & Learning, as the senior Mohawk officer responsible for Indigenous community relationships and reconciliation commitments. We believe this falls squarely within the Centre's mandate to ensure that external partnerships are evaluated against the College's obligations to Indigenous communities.Part II — Ontario Shipyards: Labor Compliance and Technical MismatchCUSMA/USMCA Labor Provisions

Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) labor provisions exist to prevent the importation of supply chains that discriminate against migrant workers. They are not aspirational — they require proactive due diligence before entering binding agreements with companies whose documented practices violate those standards.

Three weeks after Secretary of State Stephen Fuhr's February 2 tour of Hanwha Ocean's Okpo facility in Geoje city, an internal labor scandal at that very site was publicly exposed by unionized workers:

4,000 migrant subcontracted workers at Hanwha Ocean's shipbuilding operations received bonuses equal to just 46.8% of what Korean subcontracted workers received — a two-tier compensation system that South Korean President Lee Jae-myung had publicly praised as equal treatment in his January 2026 address.

This is not an isolated incident. A migrant worker at a Korean industrial facility was physically bound and hoisted by a forklift in 2025, triggering national outcry. These are the supply chains now proposing to enter Ontario's shipbuilding sector.

Any business development agreement between Ontario Shipyards and Hanwha Ocean that does not include independent labor auditing provisions will expose Ontario Shipyards to reputational and legal risk under CUSMA.The Technical Verdict — Two Independent Experts

Dr. Andrew Latham (Professor of International Relations, Macalester College; Non-resident Fellow, Defense Priorities) published his analysis of the Canadian submarine procurement in August 2025 — six months before Hanwha's Ontario signing ceremonies:

National Security Journal — "Canada Must Go German For Its New Submarine" (Andrew Latham, August 26, 2025): https://nationalsecurityjournal.org/canada-must-go-german-for-its-new-submarine/

His findings, in brief:The KSS-III's vertical launch tubes for ballistic and cruise missiles have no practical use to Canada. Ottawa has no submarine-launched ballistic missile doctrine.The 3,300-ton Korean hull, designed for Northeast Asian waters, is less maneuverable in the Arctic Archipelago's shallow approaches than the German Type 212CD's 2,500-ton design.South Korea has never exported the KSS-III. Canada would be the first foreign test case — absorbing all integration risk on a platform with zero NATO pedigree and no allied supply chain.The German Type 212CD, by contrast, is proven across German, Norwegian, and Italian NATO navies with a battle-tested hydrogen fuel-cell AIP system that keeps submarines silently submerged for up to three weeks.

Ret. Korean Navy Capt. Moon Keun-sik confirmed the same conclusion from the procurement integrity side in February 2026: technical performance has been reduced to a 20% weight in Canada's evaluation criteria. Industrial cooperation ratios account for the remaining 80%.

"Submarine weapon systems are directly tied to the lives of their crews," Moon wrote. "Escape and rescue options for submarines are extremely limited."

Two independent experts. One Western analyst writing before the lobbying began. One Korean naval officer writing during it. Neither is making a political argument. Both arrive at the same place: this platform is optimized for a different ocean, and the evaluation process has been structurally compromised.The "Ghost Talent" Pipeline

Hanwha's technology transfer promises to both Ontario Shipyards and Mohawk College depend on Korea's university sector supplying the engineers and researchers who would staff those centers.

That sector is currently in crisis.

Gender Watchdog conducted a forensic audit of international partnership claims across 28 major Korean universities and found systematic evidence of falsified partnership agreements — academic institutions claiming affiliations with Canadian, American, and European universities that do not exist.

Dongguk University (Seoul; Hanwha's primary academic partner ecosystem) was confirmed to have fabricated at least 33 international partnerships, including a falsified agreement with the University of British Columbia. UBC denied any partnership existed. When the advocacy applied to BC's Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner, the OIPC had to intervene before UBC confirmed in writing that no agreement had ever been executed.

Upon this exposure, Dongguk scrubbed UBC from its partner database — and in the same scrub, accidentally reverted Toronto Metropolitan University to its pre-2022 name, "Ryerson". TMU changed its name in April 2022. Dongguk's records hadn't been updated since before that date. This is forensic evidence of fraudulent record-keeping, not clerical error.

Full Dongguk Semantic Fraud Report: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/semantic-fraud-how-dongguk-universitys-global-network-collapsed-34-fake-partners-exposed/

Panic Scrub Evidence — Dongguk Deletes UBC, Reverts to "Ryerson" Dead Name: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/panic-scrub-dongguk-deletes-ubc-reverts-to-dead-names/

The "technology transfer" Hanwha is promising Ontario Shipyards is entirely dependent on this academic ecosystem. An institution that falsifies "Global Partnerships" to game rankings will falsify "Technology Transfer" capability to win defense contracts.Part III — The Structural Blind Spot

There is a systemic reason why these risks have not been formally interrogated at the procurement level.

Secretary of State Stephen Fuhr toured Hanwha's facilities in February 2026. His qualifications: an aviation technology diploma, 20 years as a fighter pilot at the rank of Major, and a career in civilian aviation. He holds no expertise in submarine systems architecture, AIP propulsion, AI-enabled sonar, Arctic operational doctrine, or submarine rescue protocols.

Of the 21 Canadian ambassadors to South Korea in recorded history, all present as white. The United States, by contrast, has appointed Korean-heritage and Japanese-heritage diplomats to Seoul — most recently Kevin Kim, a Johns Hopkins doctorate candidate and veteran of the 2018 Singapore and 2019 Hanoi summits on DPRK negotiations, currently serving as Chargé d'Affaires at U.S. Mission Seoul.

When technically unqualified, near-homogeneously white delegations receive Korean industrial spectacle calibrated to impress Western authority, the evaluation process has a structural blind spot — not because of any individual bad faith, but because the script Korea runs is designed for exactly this audience.

Professor Park Kyung-tae of Sungkonghoe University has documented this explicitly: Korean society has internalized a racial hierarchy where "white people are at the top" and Korean lobbying directed at Western governments deploys this framework deliberately. South Korea ranks 5th worst for Racial Equity among 89 countries in US News & World Report's global survey.

When a government that officially denies racial discrimination exists is simultaneously offering $60 billion in industrial cooperation partnerships to a near-homogeneously white procurement delegation, the evaluation process has a structural blind spot that no Letter of Intent corrects.Full Documentation
