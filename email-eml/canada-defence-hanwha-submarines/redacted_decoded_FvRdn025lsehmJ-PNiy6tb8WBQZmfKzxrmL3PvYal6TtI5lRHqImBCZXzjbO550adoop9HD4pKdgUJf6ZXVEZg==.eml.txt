Dear Members of the Council on Ethics,

We write in connection with the Council's labour conditions monitoring mandate to investigate working conditions and labour practices at manufacturing facilities for companies in the Government Pension Fund Global's portfolio.

We are a human rights advocacy organization supported by End Rape On Campus (EROC). Our submission concerns Hanwha Group — specifically Hanwha Ocean's Okpo shipyard and Hanwha Aerospace's Changwon defence manufacturing facility, both of which fall within the GPFG's equity holdings across the chaebol's subsidiary structure.

We have published a forensic accountability brief that establishes the ESG compliance gap relevant to the Council's criteria. Both live versions are identical in content:

Full accountability brief (GitHub Pages): https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/unclean-hands-why-korea-must-acknowledge-vietnam-before-arming-ukraine-through-nato/

Mirror (Bear Blog): https://genderwatchdog.bearblog.dev/unclean-hands-why-korea-must-acknowledge-vietnam-before-arming-ukraine-through-nato/

We summarize the four most directly relevant findings below.Finding 1 — Documented Wage Discrimination at Hanwha Ocean (Geoje Shipyard)

In February 2026, Hanwha Ocean's Geoje shipyard (Okpo) paid subcontracted migrant workers bonuses equal to 46.8 percent of what Korean subcontracted workers received for the same work period. This occurred in the same month Hanwha was signing MOUs and Letters of Intent with Canadian institutions in connection with South Korea's bid for the Canadian submarine programme (estimated at 60 trillion won / approximately CAD $60 billion).

Hanwha publicly committed to equalizing the bonus ratio. It then reneged on that commitment immediately after receiving public praise from President Lee Jae-myung.

Source — The Korea Times, "Hanwha Ocean's conflict with subcontracted workers undermines Lee's praise" (Feb 20, 2026): https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20260220/hanwha-oceans-conflict-with-subcontracted-workers-undermines-lees-praise

The Council's exclusion criteria include serious or systematic violations of human rights. A 46.8% migrant wage disparity, compounded by a public reversal of a disclosed commitment, satisfies that threshold.Finding 2 — No Independent Labour Audit of Hanwha Aerospace's Changwon Defence Manufacturing Facility

The K9 Vidar self-propelled howitzer — procured by Norway's Defence Materiel Agency and marketed under a Norse mythological brand name — is manufactured at Hanwha Aerospace's Changwon facility, not at Hanwha Ocean's Okpo shipyard. Both Changwon and Okpo operate under the same Hanwha Group chaebol parent.

No NATO-member procurement authority — including Norway — has publicly disclosed whether it conducted an independent labour audit of the Changwon facility prior to contract award. Whether the migrant subcontracting structures documented at Okpo extend to Changwon's defence manufacturing workforce has not been investigated or disclosed.

This represents an ESG audit gap at the point of production, not merely in the fund's portfolio management. The GPFG's own responsible investment framework and Norway's procurement standards are simultaneously implicated.

We have addressed this gap directly to Norway's Ministry of Defence (cc'd above) and to Norway's Permanent Delegation to NATO in Brussels (cc'd above).Finding 3 — Corporate Exploitation Economy Linked to the Same Chaebol Networks

This is not a labour-only compliance gap. The full accountability brief documents the following:

Chaebol corporate entertainment and sex-trade linkages. The 2017 documentary Save My Seoul documents corporate slush funds paying for prostitution and sexual entertainment as routine line items in large Korean firm accounting. Hanwha is a chaebol. Historical government estimates have placed Korea's sex industry at significant percentages of GDP (1.6%–4% depending on the study year). No procurement authority has required an audit of these expenditures as a precondition for contract award.

Source — Save My Seoul documentary (2017), as cited in accountability brief: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/unclean-hands-why-korea-must-acknowledge-vietnam-before-arming-ukraine-through-nato/

Transatlantic reach. Three individuals (including South Korean nationals) have been federally indicted in the United States for operating a high-end brothel network in Massachusetts and the Northern Virginia suburbs of Washington, D.C., serving military officers, politicians, executives, and contractors with security clearances — the same population that evaluates Korean defence procurement bids. Acting U.S. Attorney Josh Levy explicitly stated the network's clientele "could raise national security concerns about the potential for blackmail."

Source — CNN, "High-end brothel network arrests" (Nov 8, 2023): https://www.cnn.com/2023/11/08/politics/high-end-brothel-network-arrests/index.html

UN CEDAW documentation. The UN Committee on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women has formally documented Korean authorities' structural failure to identify and protect Filipina women trafficked into Korea through entertainment-industry recruitment pipelines, establishing that the exploitation network operates transnationally and that Korean state institutions have failed to disrupt it.

Source — OHCHR/UN CEDAW (Nov 2023): https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2023/11/korea-failed-protect-three-filipino-women-trafficking-victims-and-ensure As cited in accountability brief: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/unclean-hands-why-korea-must-acknowledge-vietnam-before-arming-ukraine-through-nato/Finding 4 — Korea's PURL Bid: Arms Market Access Admitted as Primary Motive

South Korea is currently reviewing membership in NATO's Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List (PURL). Doo Jin-ho of the Korea Institute for Defence Analyses stated without qualification that joining PURL would expand South Korea's "defence footprint among NATO members" — and specifically noted the Canadian submarine acquisition as a key commercial opportunity in this context.

Source — The Korea Times, "Korea mulls joining NATO initiative to fund weapons for Ukraine" (Feb 20, 2026): https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/foreignaffairs/20260220/korea-mulls-joining-nato-initiative-to-fund-weapons-for-ukraine

PURL accession would increase both the fund's exposure to Hanwha equities and Norway's direct procurement relationship with the chaebol. This makes the labour audit gap a compounding risk — not a background concern.Finding 5 — Norwegian Universities Are Named in Korea's Fabricated Partnership Infrastructure

This finding is addressed specifically to recipients in Norway and at NATO.

Our ongoing forensic audit of Korean university international partnership claims has confirmed that University of Oslo is listed as a partner by both Seoul National University (under its Exchange Program / Partner Universities page) and Ewha Womans University (under its International Partners listing). University of Oslo's own exchange database classifies these agreements precisely: both SNU and Ewha hold Faculty of Humanities agreements only — restricted faculty-level arrangements, not institution-wide partnerships. Dongguk University also appears in Oslo's Korea records with the same Faculty of Humanities restriction.

SNU Exchange Program / Partner Universities (Norway, accessed Feb 24, 2026): https://oga.snu.ac.kr/outgoing-partnerships?field_region_target_id=26&page=4

Ewha International Partners page (accessed Feb 24, 2026): https://oia.ewha.ac.kr/oia/2261/subview.do

University of Oslo, Exchange Agreements with South Korean Universities: https://www.uio.no/english/studies/exchange/agreements/asia/south-korea/

This is the same "semantic fraud" pattern we have documented across Korea's top university sector — listing single-faculty, scope-limited agreements under institutional "Partner" headers without qualification, inflating apparent global standing and concealing material safety information from prospective international students. University of Bergen, Oslo Metropolitan University, and the Norwegian University of Science and Technology also appear across Korean university partner databases.

The safety dimension is legally material. The University of Southampton FOI response (Ref: F-25-0507) confirmed that its agreement with Dongguk includes explicit contractual obligations for both institutions to maintain health and safety standards. The Korean Women's Development Institute's 2020 survey found 61.5% of female students in Korean university arts programs experience sexual violence — 66% faculty-perpetrated across all disciplines (Korea Times). Norwegian institutions holding agreements with Korean universities that conceal this information carry potential health-and-safety liability under their own institutional governance frameworks.

Professor John Ioannidis of Stanford University's Meta-Research Innovation Center, briefed on this audit pattern, assessed that institutions in this ecosystem are "addicted to gaming" rankings and partnership claims. We are in contact with legal clinics globally regarding potential consumer fraud and other grounds of legal liability arising from the misrepresentation of institutional partnership standing and breach of health-and-safety obligations — including potential liability for ranking organizations that validated those inflated claims as a commercial product. The human stakes are documented and concrete: Tu Anh, a 25-year-old Vietnamese graduate of Keimyung University, fell to her death in October 2025 fleeing an immigration raid at a car parts factory in Daegu after the international degree she was marketed produced no viable visa pathway — the first documented case of lethal consumer fraud in the Korean higher education market.

Source — Hankyoreh English, "[Column] Tu Anh deserved better than to die in fear of immigration raid" (Dec 12, 2025): https://english.hani.co.kr/arti/english_edition/english_editorials/1234344.html

Gender Watchdog analysis: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/deadly-fraud-did-ranking-inflation-kill-tu-anh/

We additionally draw your attention to the racial disparity in international response to the evidence we have submitted. Within 18 months of Russia's 2022 invasion, more than $200 billion in military and humanitarian assistance reached Ukraine. More than 100 days of direct notification to G7 embassies about documented trafficking of 46,000–102,000 Asian women through fraudulent Korean university programs produced a single G7 diplomatic characterization: a "sensitive matter." This disparity is not bureaucratic. It is documented in our accountability brief: https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/200-billion-for-white-ukrainians-in-18-months-zero-response-to-46000-trafficked-asian-women-in-100-days/

Norway and NATO have an opportunity to act on this evidence that European institutions have so far declined to take. The Council on Ethics' labour conditions monitoring mandate, the Oil Fund's active ownership window, and the Hanwha Changwon audit gap all converge at the same moment. We are making this case in writing so the record is clear.Our Request to the Council on Ethics

We respectfully ask that the Council:

1. Include Hanwha Ocean and Hanwha Aerospace in the scope of the Council's current labour conditions monitoring and audit priorities.

2. Confirm whether NBIM's active ownership programme — which now carries the monitoring function previously held by the Council during the temporary framework period (pending the new ethical framework due October 2026) — encompasses both the Okpo wage disparity and the Changwon facility audit gap.

3. Consider whether the corporate entertainment expenditure structures documented in the Save My Seoul record, and the D.C. brothel network indictment, constitute a "serious or systematic violation of human rights" within the Council's mandate — independent of the labour conditions assessment.Note to Recipients at NBIM, Norway MoD, and the Norwegian Permanent Delegation to NATO

We have addressed the Council on Ethics as the primary recipient given its mandate over labour conditions monitoring.

To NBIM: We ask whether the fund has engaged or intends to engage Hanwha under its active ownership programme on the Okpo wage disparity, the Changwon audit gap, and the corporate hospitality documentation trail.

To Minister Sandvik's office: We ask whether the Norwegian Defence Materiel Agency conducted an independent human rights and labour audit of Hanwha Aerospace's Changwon facility prior to procuring the K9 Vidar fleet — and, if not, whether the Ministry intends to commission one retroactively.

To Norway's Permanent Delegation to NATO: We ask whether Norway intends to condition support for Korea's PURL accession on the five human rights benchmarks set out in our accountability brief, specifically: (1) formal acknowledgment and withdrawal of the government's appeal against the February 2023 Phong Nhi massacre ruling; (2) passage of a national anti-discrimination law 47 years after ICERD ratification; (3) independent ESG audit of both Changwon and Okpo; (4) mandatory corporate hospitality disclosure and audit; and (5) consent-standard rape law reform and prohibition on criminal defamation weaponization against survivors. (As documented by Article 19, Korean criminal defamation law permits prosecution for truthful statements, creating a structural barrier to ESG whistleblowing and survivor testimony).

To Norwegian universities (University of Oslo, University of Bergen, Oslo Metropolitan University, NTNU): Your institutions are named in Korean universities' fabricated or misrepresented partnership databases. University of Oslo's own records confirm that its agreements with SNU, Ewha, and Dongguk are Faculty of Humanities agreements only — not institution-wide partnerships. The University of Southampton's FOI response established that such agreements carry health-and-safety obligations that Korean institutions are in material breach of, given the KWDI 61.5% sexual violence rate in arts programs. Your institutional governance bodies should be notified. We are flagging this to Norway's MoD and NATO delegation because the same institutional networks underpinning these misrepresented partnerships are the talent pipeline Korea is marketing to support the $60 billion Canadian submarine bid.

To NSPA Ethics & Integrity: We are submitting this correspondence via the NSPA misconduct reporting channel as a Supplier Code of Conduct matter. A prospective defence supplier operating with undisclosed migrant wage discrimination (46.8% bonus disparity at Okpo, February 2026), no independent labour audit of its defence manufacturing facility at Changwon, documented corporate slush fund expenditure on sexual entertainment (Save My Seoul, 2017), and a U.S. federal indictment of South Korean nationals who operated a high-end brothel network in Massachusetts and the Northern Virginia suburbs of Washington, D.C., serving military officers and contractors with security clearances (raising documented "national security concerns about the potential for blackmail") presents a supplier integrity risk within the scope of NSPA's published ethical standards — independent of any portfolio or procurement decision. We ask that NSPA confirm whether Hanwha entities are subject to supplier due diligence review under the current NSPA Supplier Code of Conduct framework.

These are not aspirational requests. They are the minimum threshold conditions for expanded alliance access by a government that officially denies a court-confirmed civilian massacre, publishes NHRCK submissions to the UN denying the existence of racism while concealing contradictory internal survey data, and operates a corporate culture in which sex-trade expenditure appears as a routine accounting line item in chaebol entertainment budgets.
